## Overview

Morrow is a personal-first, AI-assisted creative workspace that helps a creator capture unfinished thoughts, develop authentic social content, and return to work over time. Its brand promise is “Make something of today.”

Design for Possibility, Continuity, and Ownership: make beginning approachable, keep work and its source connected, and preserve the creator's voice and control. AI supports the work; it does not define the entire product identity.

The visual direction is a personal creative studio with clear typography, balanced neutral surroundings, selective acid-lime emphasis, and layered working surfaces. This foundation guides further screen design rather than prescribing a complete component system.

## Colors

Use balanced neutrals as the dominant environment: white working surfaces, near-neutral grey surroundings, and charcoal text. Keep strong cream or blue casts out of the foundation.

Use acid lime as the signature accent. Concentrate it on the main creation action and selected details so the creator's content retains visual priority.

Pair bright lime fills with dark text. For selected navigation, use a pale lime treatment with a dark readable label. Keep ordinary body text charcoal rather than applying lime to text on white.

Give secondary actions a quieter neutral treatment. Focus indicators must remain distinguishable against their adjacent surfaces; a bright lime edge alone is insufficient on white.

## Typography

Use DM Sans as the primary family for navigation, controls, headings, metadata, and writing. Its approachable character should carry through practical interface text as well as larger labels.

Establish hierarchy through role, size, weight, spacing, and grouping. Keep page headings subordinate to the actual creative task in working views.

Present short social posts as writing rather than as articles with mandatory display headlines. An internal draft name is organisational metadata and is separate from the content that will be shared.

## Layout

Make capturing an incomplete thought a direct action that does not require a title, platform, or AI request before the thought can be saved.

Give returning work a clear continuation path with enough excerpt and context to recognise it.

In the desktop Studio, place the current working draft on the left and optional AI discussion on the right. Keep the draft visually primary. The working version may combine the creator's writing and explicitly accepted suggestions.

Keep platform controls and other administration secondary to writing. Make the original idea accessible through compact source context rather than repeatedly placing a large source panel above the editor.

Keep conversation messages, proposed revisions, and saved content distinguishable. A suggestion enters the working draft through an explicit creator action.

Accommodate short posts, longer platform-specific drafts, and threads without imposing an article structure on every item. Preserve the relationships between an original idea and its independently editable platform versions.

Preserve usable writing space as the viewport narrows and keep assistance accessible without squeezing the draft into an impractical column.

## Elevation & Depth

Use layered surfaces as the default: a quiet neutral canvas with clear white working areas and fine boundaries where separation is needed.

Keep supporting lists and browsing rows flat, using spacing and dividers to establish grouping.

Reserve shadows for temporary overlays such as menus, popovers, and dialogs. Surface separation should identify the active work rather than turn every section into an elevated card.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Preserve access to the source idea after developing a draft.
- Keep capture, manual editing, saving, and content management usable without AI.
- Make save progress, successful saving, and failures understandable and actionable.
- Let the creator inspect and edit the context used to personalise assistance.
- Keep publication planning distinct from a record of actual publication.

### Don't

- Invent personal experiences or present unapproved AI opinions as the creator's own.
- Apply long-term Brand Brain changes without creator approval.
- Silently replace unrelated writing when applying a targeted revision.
- Require every idea or draft to complete a mandatory linear publishing workflow.
- Represent an internal planned date as proof that a social platform scheduled or published the content.
