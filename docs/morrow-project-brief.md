Morrow
======

**Make something of today.**

**Product Brief · Version 1.1 · October 2026**

1\. Product Vision
------------------

Build a personal-first, AI-assisted content workspace and content command centre that helps a creator consistently turn ideas, experiences, observations, and research into authentic, platform-specific content.

Morrow combines a conversational AI Studio, an idea bank, a content workspace, a content calendar, and a personalized Brand Brain in one environment.

The goal isn't simply to generate more content. It's to make the entire process of thinking, creating, organizing, and publishing content easier and more sustainable.

**Brand promise:** Morrow helps people capture their thoughts, develop their ideas, express themselves in their own voices, and create more consistently.

**Core principle:** AI should amplify the creator's thinking and voice, not replace them. AI is an enabling technology, not the product's entire identity.

### Why Morrow

The name draws inspiration from "the morrow"—tomorrow, the day that follows today. It represents possibility, progress, continuity, and the opportunity to make something meaningful from what we already have.

Every idea carries the possibility of becoming something more. An observation can become a story. An experience can become a meaningful post. A rough thought can develop into something worth sharing. Morrow helps close the gap between having something to say and actually saying it.

The name evokes tomorrow, but the product is not about postponing creativity. It's about making something of today so that tomorrow has something to build on.

### Brand philosophy

1.  **Possibility:** A creator does not need to have everything figured out before beginning. Rough thoughts can become meaningful work.
    
2.  **Continuity:** Creativity involves capturing, developing, and sharing ideas over time—not merely producing isolated pieces of content.
    
3.  **Ownership:** The creator's ideas, experiences, perspective, and voice remain central. AI should support the creative process, not replace the person creating.
    

2\. The Problem
---------------

Creating content consistently involves more than writing.

Creators must generate ideas, decide what is worth sharing, develop those ideas, adapt content for different platforms, maintain a consistent voice, plan publication, and learn from previous results.

These activities are often scattered across conversations, documents, notes, calendars, and social platforms.

Generic AI writing tools introduce another problem: their outputs can sound repetitive, artificial, or disconnected from the creator's actual experiences and opinions.

Morrow aims to bring these activities together while preserving authenticity and creator control. It is not merely a chatbot, a generic AI writer, a social scheduling dashboard, or an autonomous publishing agent.

3\. Target User
---------------

### Initial audience

One creator using the application personally.

The product will initially support a creator who:

*   Wants to publish consistently without sacrificing quality.
    
*   Generates ideas through work, learning, personal observations, and conversations.
    
*   Creates content for multiple social platforms.
    
*   Wants AI assistance that gradually becomes more aligned with their writing voice.
    
*   Needs help organizing ideas, drafts, and publishing plans.
    
*   Has little or no budget for paid AI subscriptions.
    

### Future direction

The underlying architecture should leave room for separate creator workspaces in the future.

However, team collaboration, subscriptions, elaborate onboarding, and full SaaS administration are outside the initial scope.

4\. Product Goals
-----------------

The goals are prioritized in this order:

1.  **Consistency:** Help the creator maintain a sustainable publishing routine.
    
2.  **Quality:** Produce useful, relevant, authentic content.
    
3.  **Time savings:** Reduce the time required to turn an idea into publishable content.
    
4.  **Audience growth:** Support learning and experimentation that may improve reach and engagement.
    

Audience growth is a desired outcome, not a guaranteed result.

A longer-term target is to reduce a typical content-preparation session from approximately an hour to 10–15 minutes when the task and source material permit it.

5\. Core Product Experience
---------------------------

The application will be a workspace-first dashboard with an adaptive AI Studio.

### Home

A concise daily briefing that presents:

*   Drafts needing attention.
    
*   Upcoming publication plans.
    
*   Gaps in the intended content schedule.
    
*   Relevant inspiration and possible topics.
    
*   A recommended next action.
    

The briefing should be actionable, not overwhelming.

### AI Studio

The main conversational workspace.

The user can:

*   Brainstorm and develop ideas.
    
*   Draft new content.
    
*   Refine existing writing.
    
*   Generate platform-specific variations.
    
*   Compare alternatives.
    
*   Request targeted revisions.
    
*   Save outputs to the content library.
    

Conversation and editing should coexist without making the interface unnecessarily complicated.

### Idea Bank

A place to capture raw thoughts immediately.

The system can subsequently suggest:

*   Categories and tags.
    
*   Related ideas.
    
*   Potential content angles.
    
*   Opportunities to combine ideas.
    

AI organization is optional. Original ideas must remain intact, and not every idea needs to become a post.

### Content Library

A searchable workspace for drafts and published content.

Content should support flexible statuses:

*   Idea
    
*   Draft
    
*   Ready for Review
    
*   Approved
    
*   Scheduled
    
*   Published
    
*   Archived
    

These statuses are not a mandatory linear workflow. The user remains in control of changes and publication decisions.

### Calendar

A place to plan and distribute content across platforms.

The system should consider posting frequency, draft quality, relevance, effort, and variety when suggesting what to publish next.

Recommendations must remain editable and optional.

A scheduled item in the application does not imply that the social platform itself has scheduled or published it.

### Brand Brain

The structured representation of the creator's identity and content preferences.

It will contain four primary areas:

1.  **Identity:** Background, interests, goals, and relevant experience.
    
2.  **Voice:** Writing preferences, tone, style, and examples.
    
3.  **Content Strategy:** Themes, pillars, platforms, and objectives.
    
4.  **Learning & Feedback:** Approved preferences and patterns learned from feedback.
    

The creator can inspect and edit this information.

AI-proposed long-term changes require approval.

6\. Content Creation and Personalization
----------------------------------------

The application must distinguish between:

*   Actual personal experiences.
    
*   The creator's stated opinions.
    
*   Ideas still being explored.
    
*   Facts obtained through research.
    
*   Suggestions generated by AI.
    

The system must not invent personal experiences or present unapproved AI-generated opinions as the creator's own.

### Content variations

By default, the AI should offer three meaningfully different alternatives when multiple options are useful.

The creator can compare them, combine elements, request revisions, and save preferred examples.

### Voice improvement

The system should use feedback, edits, saved examples, and recurring preferences to improve future generations.

However, a single edit must not automatically become a permanent writing preference.

Long-term changes to the Brand Brain require creator approval.

### Repetition awareness

The system should consider previously published content when proposing new ideas.

It can flag potential repetition and suggest fresh angles without preventing the creator from revisiting a topic intentionally.

7\. Platform Strategy
---------------------

The initial publishing priority is X, followed by LinkedIn and WhatsApp Status.

A central idea may produce several platform-specific versions, but content can also be created independently for each platform.

Related versions should retain their connection to the original idea.

The initial application will support manual publishing and reminders. Official publishing integrations can be considered later, where supported and feasible.

8\. AI and Budget Strategy
--------------------------

**Constraint: No planned expenditure on AI API subscriptions during initial development and personal testing.**

The product should:

*   Start with one suitable free-tier AI provider, subject to eligibility and acceptable usage terms.
    
*   Keep the AI integration replaceable.
    
*   Avoid unnecessary AI calls for saving drafts, updating statuses, or organizing content.
    
*   Use existing context and saved preferences efficiently.
    
*   Explain usage limits and service failures clearly.
    
*   Preserve access to saved work when AI is unavailable.
    
*   Keep essential manual workflows functional without AI.
    

Automatic switching between multiple providers and local AI fallback are future possibilities, not initial requirements.

Free-tier limits, provider policies, and data terms must be verified before implementation. Free access is not guaranteed to remain available indefinitely.

9\. Data Ownership and Reliability
----------------------------------

The creator's content must not become dependent on the availability of an AI provider.

The application should provide:

*   Automatic saving with visible save-state feedback.
    
*   Lightweight version history and recovery.
    
*   Export of ideas, content, and Brand Brain information.
    
*   Portable formats such as JSON and Markdown.
    
*   Clear separation between stored content and AI-generated responses.
    

Data recovery and export should be implemented in phases, beginning with reliable saving.

10\. Performance and Learning
-----------------------------

The creator may record available metrics such as views, likes, replies, and reposts.

AI-assisted reflection can help identify patterns and suggest experiments.

The system must distinguish observed results from assumptions and avoid claiming that a particular writing choice caused an outcome without sufficient evidence.

Performance analysis remains secondary to the primary content workflow.

11\. Version One Scope
----------------------

### Included

*   Personal-first dashboard.
    
*   Daily briefing.
    
*   Conversational AI Studio and editor.
    
*   Brand Brain.
    
*   Quick idea capture and organization.
    
*   Content library and search.
    
*   Platform-specific content adaptation.
    
*   Flexible content statuses.
    
*   Calendar and scheduling recommendations.
    
*   Manual publishing workflow and reminders.
    
*   Feedback-driven personalization.
    
*   Basic performance tracking.
    
*   Automatic saving.
    
*   Lightweight version history.
    
*   Content export and backup.
    

### Excluded initially

*   Autonomous background agents.
    
*   Automatic publishing and complex platform integrations.
    
*   Multi-user collaboration.
    
*   Subscription and billing systems.
    
*   Elaborate analytics dashboards.
    
*   AI performance predictions.
    
*   Complex adaptive scheduling.
    
*   Multiple-provider orchestration.
    
*   Custom model training.
    
*   An extensive plugin ecosystem.
    

12\. Success Criteria
---------------------

The first version should demonstrate that the creator can:

1.  Capture an idea without interrupting their workflow.
    
2.  Develop it into a useful draft with or without AI.
    
3.  Produce appropriate versions for different platforms.
    
4.  Maintain a consistent voice using the Brand Brain.
    
5.  Organize drafts and plan publication.
    
6.  Identify a sensible next action through the daily briefing.
    
7.  Recover previous work and export important data.
    
8.  Continue managing content when the AI service is unavailable.
    

Success should initially be evaluated through actual personal use, not assumptions about what future users might want.

13\. Product Principles
-----------------------

Brand philosophy (Possibility, Continuity, Ownership) sits alongside these operating principles:

*   **Authenticity over generic polish.**
    
*   **Consistency without forced quotas.**
    
*   **Useful assistance over unnecessary automation.**
    
*   **Creator control over autonomous decisions.**
    
*   **Simple capture over administrative overhead.**
    
*   **Selective memory over indiscriminate retention.**
    
*   **Reliable manual workflows when AI fails.**
    
*   **Data ownership over provider lock-in.**
    
*   **Working functionality over feature accumulation.**
    
*   **The interface organizes the work; the AI helps move it forward.**
    

14\. Next Steps
---------------

The Product Requirements Document, Technical Architecture, and UX/UI Design Specification already extend this brief.

The next implementation phase should establish a working product foundation (workspace shell, reliable saving, and documented environment setup), then validate the zero-budget AI provider strategy before depending on it for core creation workflows.
